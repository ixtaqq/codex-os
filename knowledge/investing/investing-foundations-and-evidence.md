# Investing foundations and evidence

## Scope and safety

This is general education, not personalized financial, legal, or tax advice and not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. It deliberately avoids current security picks and return forecasts. Suitability depends on goals, time horizon, cash-flow needs, liabilities, risk capacity, tax residence, account rules, and ability to tolerate loss. Laws, product terms, tax treatment, and approved-provider lists change; verify them from the relevant Malaysian authority before acting.

## Executive summary

The durable core of investing is simple but not easy. Evidence in this note is labeled implicitly by type: mathematical identities (compounding and fee arithmetic), foundational models (portfolio theory), historical empirical findings (factors and manager/trader performance), and regulatory risk guidance. Only the first category is mechanically true; the others require assumptions, interpretation, or current verification.

1. Define the goal, horizon, liquidity need, and maximum loss you can actually tolerate before choosing products.
2. Keep emergency and near-term spending money out of volatile assets.
3. Use broad diversification across issuers, sectors, countries, currencies, and—when suitable—asset classes. Diversification reduces avoidable concentration risk but cannot eliminate market loss ([Investor.gov: asset allocation and diversification](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/asset-allocation)).
4. Select an asset allocation whose drawdowns you can survive without abandoning it. Rebalance by a written rule rather than by headlines.
5. Treat costs, taxes, spreads, currency conversion, and behavior as certain drags; expected outperformance is uncertain. Compare total cost, not just a fund's stated expense ratio ([Investor.gov: fees](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/understanding-fees)).
6. Regard leverage, derivatives, concentrated bets, illiquid assets, and crypto as specialist risk budgets, not substitutes for a diversified core.
7. Verify every intermediary and offer. In Malaysia, check the Securities Commission Malaysia (SC) public register and Investor Alert List before sending money ([SC public register](https://www.sc.com.my/regulation/licensing/public-register-of-licence-holders-and-registered-persons), [SC Investor Alert List](https://www.sc.com.my/investor-alert-list)).

## 1. What investing is—and is not

An investment exchanges current purchasing power for uncertain future cash flows or resale value. Return can come from income (interest, dividends, rent), growth in underlying cash flows, change in valuation, or currency movement. Nominal return is measured in money; real return is approximately nominal return minus inflation. What matters for a goal is the after-fee, after-tax, inflation-adjusted result.

Compounding means returns themselves can earn returns. For a lump sum, future value is `principal × (1 + return)^years`; volatile returns compound geometrically, so the order and depth of losses matter. A 50% loss requires a 100% gain to recover. Investor.gov explains compound growth and the benefit of time, while making clear that illustrative rates are assumptions rather than guarantees ([Investor.gov: introduction to investing](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing)).

Saving and investing serve different horizons. Cash and insured deposits emphasize liquidity and nominal stability; growth assets accept uncertainty for higher expected long-run return. Money required soon should not depend on selling a volatile asset at a favorable price.

## 2. Risk is more than volatility

Useful risk categories include:

- **Permanent loss:** business failure, fraud, default, dilution, overpayment, or forced sale.
- **Market risk:** a broad market decline that diversification within that market cannot remove.
- **Concentration risk:** excessive exposure to one issuer, sector, country, style, or narrative.
- **Sequence risk:** withdrawals during early losses can permanently impair a portfolio even when its long-run average return is acceptable.
- **Inflation risk:** safe-looking nominal assets may lose purchasing power.
- **Interest-rate and duration risk:** bond prices generally move opposite market yields; greater duration means greater sensitivity ([FINRA: bonds and duration risk](https://www.finra.org/investors/investing/investment-products/bonds)).
- **Credit risk:** an issuer may delay, reduce, or fail to make promised payments.
- **Liquidity risk:** an asset may be costly or impossible to sell quickly near its quoted value.
- **Currency risk:** returns in a foreign currency can be amplified or reduced when converted to Malaysian ringgit.
- **Leverage and margin risk:** borrowed exposure magnifies gains and losses and can force liquidation.
- **Operational/custody risk:** errors, cyberattack, weak controls, or insolvency at an intermediary can impair access.
- **Behavioral risk:** panic selling, performance chasing, overtrading, confirmation bias, and overconfidence can turn tolerable market risk into permanent loss.

Risk tolerance is willingness to bear loss; risk capacity is the financial ability to do so. Capacity should dominate. A long horizon can support volatility only if the investor will not need the funds and can stay invested.

## 3. Diversification and portfolio mathematics

Harry Markowitz's foundational portfolio-selection framework showed that portfolio risk depends not only on each holding's risk but also on how holdings move together. Combining imperfectly correlated assets can improve the expected-return/risk trade-off ([Markowitz, 1952, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1952.tb01525.x)). This does not make optimization estimates reliable: expected returns, volatility, and correlations are uncertain and change over time.

Practical diversification therefore favors robust breadth over precision:

- diversify within an asset class, not merely across labels;
- inspect overlapping fund holdings—several funds can conceal the same concentration;
- diversify globally when access, cost, tax, and currency considerations permit;
- avoid assuming correlations remain low during crises;
- remember that a narrow sector or thematic ETF is still concentrated.

Diversification reduces idiosyncratic risk; it does not prevent loss when most risky assets fall together. Market-cap-weighted funds are broad but can still become concentrated in the largest companies, countries, or sectors. Read the index methodology and current holdings.

## 4. Major asset classes

### Cash and money-market instruments

Cash, deposits, Treasury bills, and money-market funds support emergencies and short-horizon liabilities. Risks include inflation, reinvestment at lower rates, bank or fund structure, currency, and deposit-insurance limits. A money-market fund is an investment fund, not automatically the same thing as an insured bank deposit. For Malaysia, verify eligible deposits and limits directly with Perbadanan Insurans Deposit Malaysia ([PIDM deposit insurance](https://www.pidm.gov.my/general/how-we-protect-you/deposit-insurance-system)).

### Bonds and sukuk

A bond is a loan to an issuer; sukuk are Shariah-compliant certificates whose contractual structure and risks must be read rather than assumed from the label. Key variables are yield, maturity, duration, seniority, collateral, call features, credit quality, liquidity, currency, and tax treatment.

Bond prices generally fall when required yields rise. Duration gives an approximate percentage price sensitivity to a one-percentage-point yield change; convexity makes the relationship nonlinear. A bond fund has no single maturity at which the investor is guaranteed principal back. Yield to maturity is conditional on holding, promised payments, and reinvestment assumptions; it is not a guarantee. Higher yield usually compensates for some combination of credit, duration, liquidity, call, currency, or structural risk—not free return ([FINRA: bonds](https://www.finra.org/investors/investing/investment-products/bonds)).

### Public equities

A share is a residual ownership claim. Equity holders benefit if the business grows distributable cash flows, but stand behind creditors and can lose the full investment. Long-run return is driven by dividends, per-share earnings/cash-flow growth, and valuation change. A sound company can be a poor investment at an excessive price, and a low valuation can reflect genuine deterioration.

Individual-stock analysis requires business economics, competitive position, management incentives, accounting quality, balance-sheet resilience, dilution, cyclicality, valuation, and adverse scenarios. Diversified funds reduce company-specific research burden but do not remove market risk.

### Property and REITs

Direct property combines a real asset with high transaction costs, concentration, maintenance, illiquidity, and often leverage. Real estate investment trusts (REITs) offer traded exposure but introduce market-price volatility, management and governance risk, interest-rate sensitivity, tenant/sector concentration, and fund expenses. Rental yield should be calculated after vacancy, maintenance, taxes, insurance, financing, and transaction costs.

### Commodities and gold

Commodities do not produce contractual cash flow. Return depends on spot-price change and, for futures-based products, collateral yield and the shape/roll of the futures curve. Gold can behave differently from stocks and bonds in some regimes, but can also suffer long real drawdowns. Product structure, custody, spread, and tracking matter.

### Private assets

Private equity, venture capital, private credit, and unlisted property may offer differentiated exposures but have opaque valuations, limited liquidity, complex fees, selection bias, leverage, and restricted access. Smoothed appraisals can make reported volatility look lower than economic risk. Demand audited statements, cash-flow transparency, valuation policy, legal rights, conflict disclosures, and a realistic exit path.

### Cryptoassets

Cryptoassets vary widely and may involve price volatility, custody/key loss, smart-contract and bridge failure, market manipulation, platform insolvency, unclear legal claims, and regulatory change. A token's scarcity or price history does not establish intrinsic value. Never infer legitimacy from an app, influencer, or exchange listing. Malaysia's SC regulates recognized-market digital asset operators and publishes regulated-operator information; verify current status directly ([SC digital assets](https://www.sc.com.my/digital-assets)). Treat any exposure as loss-capable speculative capital, with custody and counterparty risks explicitly understood.

## 5. Asset allocation and portfolio construction

Asset allocation divides a portfolio among assets such as equities, fixed income, and cash. Investor.gov emphasizes time horizon and risk tolerance and describes periodic or threshold rebalancing ([Investor.gov: asset allocation](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/asset-allocation)). A practical sequence is:

1. **Map liabilities:** amount, currency, earliest date, flexibility, and consequence of shortfall.
2. **Reserve liquidity:** emergency fund and known near-term spending.
3. **Set risk limits:** tolerable portfolio drawdown, concentration limits, leverage rule, and minimum liquidity.
4. **Choose strategic allocation:** broad long-run weights, expressed as ranges rather than false precision.
5. **Select implementation:** low-cost, transparent, liquid instruments whose index and domicile are understood.
6. **Rebalance:** use new contributions first; otherwise rebalance at a fixed interval or when weights cross written bands, considering costs and taxes.
7. **Review the plan, not recent winners:** change allocation when goals, capacity, horizon, liabilities, or evidence change—not merely because prices moved.

A useful structure is a **core** of broad, diversified, low-cost exposures plus an explicitly capped **satellite** allocation for active, factor, thematic, or speculative views. The cap prevents a side thesis from silently becoming the portfolio.

Dollar-cost averaging is a contribution schedule, not a guarantee against loss. For already-available cash, delaying investment changes market exposure and may reduce or increase eventual wealth; the right approach depends on risk tolerance and implementation discipline, not a claim that one schedule always wins.

## 6. Passive, active, and factor investing

### Passive and active

An index fund follows rules; it is not “no decision.” Index provider, inclusion rules, weighting, reconstitution, turnover, securities lending, domicile, and tracking difference all matter. A passive fund delivers the chosen index minus costs and implementation effects, not “the market” in the abstract.

Active management can outperform before or after costs, but identifying future outperformers in advance is difficult. Sharpe's arithmetic shows that, before costs, the asset-weighted active dollar must equal the passive market dollar; after higher active costs, aggregate active performance must lag—without implying that every manager underperforms ([Sharpe, 1991](https://www.web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/art/active/active.htm)). In Carhart's survivor-bias-free mutual-fund sample, common factors and expenses explained most persistence, and one-year persistence did not require stock-picking skill ([Carhart, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1997.tb03808.x)). S&P Dow Jones Indices' SPIVA scorecards repeatedly document substantial benchmark underperformance among active funds; results vary by category and period and the scorecard is an index provider's analysis, not a theorem ([SPIVA U.S. year-end 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-us)).

The evidence supports a default burden of proof: an active strategy should explain its edge, capacity, fees, turnover, tax impact, benchmark, risks, and why the edge should persist after publication and capital inflows. Short track records and backtests are weak evidence.

### Factors

The Fama–French research documented common return patterns associated with broad market, size, and value exposures ([Fama and French, 1993](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(93)90023-5)); Carhart added momentum in performance attribution. Factors are not guaranteed bonuses. They can underperform for years, definitions differ, premiums may shrink, and real products incur fees, taxes, turnover, trading costs, and behavioral abandonment.

Before using a factor product, examine:

- economic rationale and evidence across markets and periods;
- whether the signal survives realistic implementation costs;
- index construction, constraints, turnover, and concentration;
- overlap with existing holdings;
- the investor's ability to tolerate long relative underperformance.

## 7. Valuation and return expectations

Valuation compares price with future cash flows, assets, or normalized earnings. Common tools—discounted cash flow, earnings yield, free-cash-flow yield, price/book, dividend yield, credit spread, and real yield—depend on assumptions and are best used as ranges and scenarios.

For a discounted-cash-flow model, explicitly stress revenue growth, margins, reinvestment, dilution, terminal growth, and discount rate. Terminal value often dominates, making false precision dangerous. Relative multiples require genuinely comparable business quality, leverage, cyclicality, accounting, and growth. Low valuation alone is not a catalyst; high valuation embeds demanding expectations but does not identify timing.

Expected returns should be ranges, not single-point forecasts. Separate:

- **base rate:** long-run historical or structural evidence;
- **starting valuation/yield:** what is embedded today;
- **scenario:** optimistic, base, and adverse paths;
- **uncertainty:** model and parameter error.

Past performance is not a promise. Backtests are vulnerable to selection, look-ahead, survivorship, data-mining, and publication bias.

## 8. Funds and ETFs: what to inspect

Mutual funds pool investor assets and transact at calculated net asset value under their rules. ETFs generally trade intraday at market prices; creation/redemption arbitrage usually helps keep price near net asset value, but premiums/discounts and spreads can widen, especially in stressed or illiquid markets ([SEC: ETF investor bulletin](https://www.sec.gov/file/etfspdf)).

Read the prospectus, product highlights sheet, annual report, index methodology, and holdings. Check:

- investment objective and actual benchmark;
- physical versus synthetic replication and counterparty exposure;
- total expense ratio plus tracking difference;
- brokerage commission, bid–ask spread, currency conversion, platform/custody fees, withholding taxes, and transfer/exit fees;
- assets under management, trading liquidity, underlying-market liquidity, and closure policy;
- domicile, legal structure, tax treatment, estate-tax exposure, and investor protections;
- securities-lending policy and revenue split;
- concentration, derivatives use, leverage, and rebalancing turnover;
- distributing versus accumulating treatment and whether distributions are income, gains, or return of capital.

Fund expenses are deducted from assets and reduce return. Standardized fee tables appear near the front of U.S. fund prospectuses; other jurisdictions have equivalent disclosures. Small annual cost differences compound materially ([Investor.gov: mutual-fund and ETF fees](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/mutual-fund-fees-and-expenses)).

## 9. Trading, leverage, derivatives, and behavioral hazards

Trading adds spread, market impact, commissions, currency costs, taxes, and decision opportunities. Online execution is fast, but due diligence is not; verify order type, quantity, venue, currency, and execution status ([Investor.gov: online investing](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/investing-your-own/online-investing)). Market orders prioritize execution, not price; limit orders constrain price but may not execute.

Historical household-account evidence supports taking turnover seriously: Barber and Odean found that the heaviest traders in a large discount-broker sample substantially underperformed, consistent with trading costs and overconfidence harming results ([Barber and Odean, 2000](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00226)). This is historical evidence from one market and period, not proof that every trade is harmful.

Margin can create losses beyond deposited capital and forced sales at the worst time. Leveraged and inverse ETFs usually target a daily multiple; compounding can make longer-period returns diverge sharply from the headline multiple, especially in volatile markets ([SEC/FINRA alert on leveraged and inverse ETFs](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/leveraged-and-inverse-etfs-specialized-products-extra-risks-buy-and-hold-investors)).

Options and futures are contracts, not inherently hedges. Their payoff depends on strike, maturity, volatility, rates, path, margin, liquidity, and counterparty/clearing terms. Option buyers can lose the premium; some option sellers and leveraged futures positions can face losses larger than initial collateral. Understand the maximum loss, assignment/exercise, settlement, margin calls, and scenario behavior before use.

Behavioral safeguards:

- write the thesis, disconfirming evidence, position limit, and exit/review rule before purchase;
- wait before acting on urgency or social-media excitement;
- measure performance against an appropriate total-return benchmark after all costs;
- keep a decision journal, including rejected ideas;
- automate contributions and rebalancing where suitable;
- never average down solely because price fell;
- avoid checking long-horizon portfolios at a frequency that provokes short-horizon decisions.

## 10. Malaysia-specific implementation and regulatory checks

### Verify the provider and product

Use the SC public register to verify licensed capital-market intermediaries and registered persons, and check the SC Investor Alert List for unauthorized entities or schemes ([SC licensed intermediaries](https://www.sc.com.my/regulation/enforcement/investor-alerts/list-of-licenced-intermediaries), [SC Investor Alert List](https://www.sc.com.my/investor-alert-list)). The alert list is non-exhaustive; absence is not approval. Use Bursa Malaysia's investor education and product pages to understand listed-market rules and instruments ([Bursa Malaysia: investor education](https://www.bursamarketplace.com/)). Type official addresses yourself because cloned public-register sites and impersonation accounts can reproduce names and logos ([SC cloned-register warning](https://www.sc.com.my/resources/media/media-release/sc-warns-public-on-cloned-public-register-scam)).

Do not transfer investment money to an individual's, agent's, or unrelated bank account merely because someone claims to represent a licensed firm. Independently obtain the firm's official contact information and confirm instructions. Promises of guaranteed high returns, secret methods, limited-time pressure, recruitment rewards, remote-access requests, advance “tax” or “administration” fees to release withdrawals, and crypto-only payment are major warning signs ([SC Scam Meter](https://www.sc.com.my/investor-empowerment/scam-meter)). Report suspected scams to the relevant institution and authorities; Malaysia's National Scam Response Centre can be reached at 997 according to official government guidance ([Malaysia government: NSRC](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/portal/content/30918)). Eligible capital-market disputes against a SIDREC member may fall within SIDREC's dispute-resolution schemes; check current eligibility and monetary limits ([SIDREC schemes](https://www.sidrec.com.my/dispute-resolution-schemes/)).

### Local market and custody questions

For Bursa-listed securities, investors generally need a trading account and a Central Depository System (CDS) account through an Authorised Depository Agent; understand whether holdings are in your own CDS account or nominee custody and how that affects voting, corporate actions, statements, transfers, and insolvency handling ([Bursa: what is CDS](https://assist.bursamalaysia.com/hc/en-us/articles/12222349887247-What-is-a-Central-Depository-System-CDS), [Bursa: opening a CDS account](https://assist.bursamalaysia.com/hc/en-us/articles/12233120783375-How-do-I-open-a-Central-Depository-System-CDS-account)). Read the broker's terms for cash segregation, foreign sub-custodians, securities lending, fees, and dormant/transfer procedures. For foreign securities, identify the legal owner in the custody chain and which compensation or insolvency regime—if any—could apply.

### Ringgit and foreign exposure

A Malaysian investor's spending liabilities are often in MYR, while global assets may be denominated in other currencies. Currency exposure can diversify local economic risk but also add volatility. Fund trading currency, asset currency, and hedging policy are different concepts. A USD-traded ETF holding Japanese and European shares is not simply “USD exposure.” Read the portfolio and hedge methodology.

### Tax and account caveat—must be refreshed

Tax outcomes depend on residence, legal ownership, instrument, source of income, trading frequency/business characterization, fund domicile, treaties, withholding, and law at the time. Malaysia has changed rules affecting dividends, foreign-sourced income, and specified capital gains in recent years. For example, the official YA 2025 individual notes describe a 2% tax on taxable Malaysian dividend income above RM100,000, subject to detailed scope and exclusions ([HASiL YA 2025 explanatory notes](https://www.hasil.gov.my/media/pshpbomm/explanatorynotes_be2025_2.pdf)). HASiL's June 2024 foreign-income guidance describes a conditional, date-limited exemption through 31 December 2026 for qualifying foreign income received in Malaysia by resident individuals, with detailed “subjected to tax” rules ([HASiL foreign-income guidance](https://www.hasil.gov.my/media/fzofh1gz/20240620-guidelines-tax-treatment-in-relation-to-income-received-from-abroad-amendment-june-2024.pdf)). These examples must not be generalized to a particular taxpayer. Do not rely on an old blog or this note for a transaction. Verify current legislation, exemption orders, filing guidance, and effective dates with HASiL and obtain qualified advice for material amounts ([HASiL official portal](https://www.hasil.gov.my/)).

Foreign assets can trigger source-country withholding and estate/inheritance exposure even where Malaysia does not impose the same tax. Accumulating funds do not necessarily eliminate underlying withholding. Keep transaction, distribution, foreign-exchange, fee, and tax documents.

For retirement assets, use only current official rules for the Employees Provident Fund and any private retirement scheme; contribution, withdrawal, nomination, tax-relief, and investment-option rules can change ([EPF official site](https://www.kwsp.gov.my/), [Private Pension Administrator Malaysia](https://www.ppa.my/)).

## 11. A repeatable evidence-based workflow

### A. Write an investment policy statement

Record:

- goals, target amounts, dates, and currencies;
- emergency reserve and near-term liabilities;
- strategic asset-allocation ranges;
- maximum issuer, sector, country, and speculative allocations;
- permitted instruments and prohibited leverage;
- contribution, rebalancing, and withdrawal rules;
- benchmark and review frequency;
- conditions that justify changing the plan.

### B. Build an evidence hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. law, regulation, official registers, prospectuses, audited reports, exchange filings, and index methodologies;
2. original peer-reviewed papers and robust replications;
3. official statistical series and central-bank publications;
4. reputable secondary synthesis;
5. commentary and social media only as leads to verify.

### C. Perform product due diligence

For each product, answer:

1. What exactly do I legally own, and who holds it?
2. What return source is expected, and what must go right?
3. What can cause permanent loss or forced sale?
4. What are all recurring and transaction costs?
5. What is the benchmark, and how has tracking difference behaved?
6. What are liquidity, spread, currency, tax, domicile, and closure risks?
7. Is the provider licensed, and are documents independently verifiable?
8. What simpler, broader, or cheaper alternative achieves the same purpose?
9. What evidence would falsify the thesis?
10. How does the position affect total-portfolio exposure rather than looking attractive alone?

### D. Monitor without chasing noise

Review goals and allocation on a scheduled basis. Update immediately for material life, liability, custody, regulatory, product-structure, or tax changes. Do not change a strategic plan solely because an asset recently outperformed or underperformed. Compare outcomes after fees, taxes, contributions, withdrawals, and currency conversion.

## 12. Common claims that require skepticism

- **“Guaranteed” high return:** return above safe rates requires a risk, subsidy, or deception that must be identified.
- **“Low risk because it paid consistently”:** smooth reported returns may reflect stale marks, illiquidity, leverage, or fabricated statements.
- **“Diversified because I own many funds”:** holdings can overlap heavily.
- **“The dividend is free income”:** distributions reduce the value remaining in the asset and can be cut; total return matters.
- **“A bond fund is cash”:** bond funds have duration, credit, spread, and liquidity risk.
- **“An ETF is automatically safe and cheap”:** ETFs range from broad low-cost funds to leveraged, synthetic, concentrated, illiquid products.
- **“A backtest proves the strategy”:** data-mining and implementation gaps can make simulated results unattainable.
- **“A regulated platform means every product is safe”:** regulation may address conduct or access, not guarantee value or prevent loss.
- **“This time is different”:** sometimes regimes do change, but the burden is to specify the mechanism, evidence, valuation, and failure case.

## Conclusions

The strongest general-purpose approach is process-driven: match assets to liabilities, diversify broadly, keep total costs low, use realistic return assumptions, rebalance by rule, and reserve complexity for risks that are understood and deliberately sized. The academic evidence does not eliminate judgment; it shifts judgment away from forecasts and toward portfolio design, implementation quality, and behavior.

For a Malaysia-based investor, product suitability cannot be separated from MYR liabilities, intermediary licensing, custody chain, fund domicile, foreign withholding/estate exposure, and current Malaysian tax rules. Those facts must be checked at decision time.

## Research limitations

- This synthesis is broad, not exhaustive; specialized areas such as Islamic-finance structuring, private-market legal terms, insurance-linked investments, retirement decumulation, and cross-border estate planning require dedicated notes and professional advice.
- Foundational papers establish models or historical empirical findings, not immutable laws. Markets, products, costs, and regulation evolve.
- U.S. regulator material explains widely used concepts but does not govern Malaysian accounts. Malaysian authorities and product documents control locally.
- The regulatory and tax links were accessed on 2026-08-11; current effective law and provider status must be rechecked before action.
